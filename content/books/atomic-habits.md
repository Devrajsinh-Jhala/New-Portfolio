## What it is

James Clear's argument is that results come from small things done repeatedly, not from big decisions. The book is a practical manual for building the habits you want and dropping the ones you don't, and it hangs on one loop: cue, craving, response, reward.

## What stayed with me

- **Systems over goals.** A goal sets the direction; the system is what you do every day. People who succeed and people who don't often have the same goal, so the goal can't be what separates them.
- **One percent compounds.** Small improvements look like nothing for a long time, and then they don't. Clear calls the quiet stretch the plateau of latent potential. It is where most people stop.
- **Identity comes first.** Don't aim to read a book; become a reader. Every action is a vote for the kind of person you are becoming.
- **The four laws.** Make it obvious, make it attractive, make it easy, make it satisfying. To break a habit, turn each one around.
- **Make starting tiny.** The two-minute rule: shrink a new habit until it takes two minutes. A habit has to exist before it can be improved.
- **Never miss twice.** Missing once is an accident. Missing twice is the start of a new habit.
- **Environment beats willpower.** Set the room up so that the right thing is also the easy thing.

## Where it shows up for me

It rhymes with the levels on this site: nobody jumps from level one to level six, and each level is mostly made of ordinary days. It is also how software gets maintained. A package stays alive through small, regular releases, not through one heroic rewrite.

## Who I'd hand it to

Anyone who keeps setting goals and is tired of restarting. Read it with one habit in mind, not ten.
