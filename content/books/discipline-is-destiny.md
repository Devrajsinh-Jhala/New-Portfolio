## What it is

The second book in Ryan Holiday's series on the four Stoic virtues. This one is about temperance, which he reads as self-discipline. It is told in short chapters about people, among them Lou Gehrig, Queen Elizabeth II, Marcus Aurelius and Toni Morrison, and it moves in three parts: the body, the temperament, and the soul.

## What stayed with me

- **Discipline is a kind of freedom.** Someone who can't say no to themselves isn't free. They are run by whatever they happen to want in the moment.
- **Show up.** Lou Gehrig played 2,130 games in a row. Not every one of them was great. The streak was the achievement.
- **Start early, and do the hard thing first.** Toni Morrison wrote before dawn, before her children woke and before her day job began.
- **Rest is part of it.** Sleep, pacing, stopping before you break. Discipline that burns you out was only intensity.
- **Strict with yourself, tolerant with others.** The standard is for you. Holding everyone else to it is just being difficult.
- **The small things are the whole thing.** Being on time, keeping your space in order, finishing what you said you would. Nobody is disciplined about the big things and careless about the rest.
- **The highest form is temperament.** Decades of composure, power that doesn't change you, staying kind when you could get away with not being.

## Where it sits for me

It reads well next to Atomic Habits. Clear tells you how to make a habit stick; Holiday tells you why it is worth the trouble, and what it looks like in someone who kept it up for a lifetime.

## Who I'd hand it to

Someone who is already motivated and still inconsistent. Read a chapter a day; it is built for that.
